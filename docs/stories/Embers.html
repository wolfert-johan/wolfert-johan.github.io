Sun, Wind, and Embers

Long ago, the Sun told the Wind not to cradle the Embers of fire.

“Do not so much as think of it” spoke the Sun.

But when the wood was lit, and the embers rose, they danced in the wind with a flickering light and so did the timid wind dance with the embers. In caverns, where fires are lit, the sun’s light reaches not, and so the sun did not know.

The hot and great sun shone, and the wind tiptoed out of this cave carrying his bag which were his plans, but it was a bag of tricks. It was a bag of apples and worms, flesh and maggots, made of silk and moths, so it fell astray into many parts. The wind cried and it rained and the fires were put out. That only made the wind cry more and so the wind blew away the embers, which made him cry more again. Oh, the wind would tiptoe with tapshoes, or fly without wings, and his greatest mistake was his delusion.

Long ago, the wind spoke to God, “Dear Lord, grant me speed, and power, and wit, and, in exchange, I will give you my eyes.” And the wind saw God and saw that it was himself from very long ago, and God agreed. So the wind was fast, strong, and witty, but he was blind.

The wind is blind, a babbling buffoon. Woe to the wind! Woe that the wind travel over the sea and the land aimless, for he seems a stupid fool. Lick your finger and hold it to the air to see that the wind is a nervous and untamed beast. Hold your hand over fire to know that the wind wants the hot and the heat, but watch the embers rise to love how the wind needs more to dance, oh how he wishes to dance, but he gave his eyes and can find fire not.

Woe on the wind! He brings the words of the sun no longer, for he declared it to be night. The wind loves night! For, in day, the embers might rise and be swallowed by the sun, and the wind could not dance. The wind fears daytime, for this wind who wants to dance could never want the embers to be swallowed by the sun.

This wind with no eyes! He can declare it night, but embers still rise to the sun in the sky.

This wind fails.

This wind fails to keep embers safe, for a gust brings them into and drowns them in sea. He fails to dance without the heat of the fire. He fails to dance because embers are a hot thing, and like iron to a magnet, like hammer to an anvil, or the rushing blood to an appendage, the wind is to heat.

But this thought, above all, this question and roadblock, the wind fears this option most: that the embers should rise to the sun.

So, like a dog on a leash, he would work to not make it true.
