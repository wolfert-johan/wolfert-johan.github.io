Common Man, Psychosis Man

There is a man, the common man, perhaps like you or me, who sits or walks. I know not his face, his kindness or rudeness. No, I know not this man very well, for he is big. Big like the government over bureaucrats, or marriage over couples, or whiteness over white people. He is big over us, too. As you or I might sit on a long fence that says “woman” on one end and “man” on the other, he does, too, but where one finds me or you at one post, he sits everywhere all at once, and some places more than others. In this way he is big and wide, tall and pokey.

So I know only little about such a shadow, for how could I find his place when he is a sea of places! But in vibrant green does a snake around his mysteried neck and body shine! Written upon it, “In God we Trust.” Yes, and this is what lie the snake whispers in his ear: “It is the rout which bring in their filth that clog your throat and choke you.” Who is the rout this snake speaks of?

Our common man falls into a trance from the hateful breath of the snake, and in his vision of the rout he possesses kaleidoscope eyes. What does he see? A confusion of portals & windows that show nothing but plays! A masterful art that brings him to anger.

Here he sees a guerilla, whose skin is black like the night. Its brutish language, orcish grunts. But more than that, it speaks in violence & anger. "Oh this savage thing! Its widened nose made for the animal breaths released during the the raping and murdering of my people! Fear is all I have for this foreign threat! Never should we let them in our homes or on our streets! Like a bird I will take my flight from such creatures!" But, in truth, like a mosquito on his skin does he deal with them, because he listens to the lies of Snakes.

There he sees a freak, costumed in the way of deviance. Its sexual mutations are painted with the brush of disgust. All that is unnatural and taboo are prised like gold on a pedestal. "Oh this savage thing! Its horrid sin, such perversion, ideology, & lack of shame be the poison that molests our children with its dirty thoughts! Fear is all I have for this foreign threat! Eradication and suppression, the spray I use on this bug.” But, in truth, even our common man is a freak in his heart, and his every denial is a stab into his throbbing chest.

Now he sees a desert rat. Over it, the crescent moon. Strapped to its chest is a machine of war or implements of violence. “Oh this savage thing! Its dirty litter infests my streets and invades my home! With it, it brings a plague which kills our culture and replaces it with buboes of crime and degeneracy. Freedom is nothing to a religion that demands a submission it uses to make war against us. Fear is all I have for this foreign enemy! War and deportation are the panacea to their sickness.” But, in truth, it is war and deportation which keep these welts inflamed.

Look at the man in this trance. He is in a sorry state. Adrenaline pumps through his blood by the fury he hurls at all he is convinced will attack him, but he fights phantom foes. Despite his lashings, his hands do nothing to bring the snake off his neck, so it is a useless fear! A fear that sedates.

Look at the man in this trance. He is dull & stupid. In his bouts of psychosis he builds empires of fantasy which he walks through as though they were normal city streets. In this labyrinth he is a mouse, too ignorant to recognize the cage he has built. In every brick he lays, a clay assumption buries the well-known truth.

At every system, the snake would have him blame with rage in his hearts the enemy and the criminal. But blame is not a thing which is sponged and drained into some! Blame is a sea that the snake and criminal swim in together, but he would have our man not believe it! In this way our friend is blinded and deafened and controlled.

If only our friend would take a moment of zen. He would feel, maybe, the tears running down his cheeks, for our friend is crying. He cries and cries, for he is hurting. He cries and cries, for there is this snake on his neck and soon he must die. He cries and cries, for the system is broken and serves him not. He would see, perhaps, and with all his vision, a snake the color of power wrapping around his neck, if only he would open his eyes!
