Tea

Me and my roommate have been living in this house that’s out in nowhere.         It’s a place where the daisies poke up from the cracked concrete and the cars pass like no one’s driving. It’s a place where a long path through the light forest brings you to a long and endless sea. It’s a place where the stillness of the air whispers sweet lies in the sound of the 70’s. Vignettes are trapped in the grain of an old film camera that didn’t exist before it was found. Memories are made like snowflakes coming down onto your nose when you’re lonely. Cigarette ashes are filling a windowsill and they’ve been there for years.

Dancing fairies blessed such a place a long time ago with funny little things. They paired little funny boys with little funny lies to tell them. They paired madness with love and sadness with intimacy. They wound promise, like a vine, with betrayal and ignorance with knowing.

One night I woke up. Oh God! Those two teapots I had put on the stove those nights ago were simmering and screeching with heavy pressure. They sang like a chorus of upset angels, panging in my head. Now I had woken up in this house again where I left my teapots and dear, what a mess all this spilling water was making.

        Plus, the old teabags had been sitting for so long and, really, the tea should’ve been ready by now. “My roommate really wanted to sip some tea this morning” said I, “though I think that is a rather dreary and upsetting idea!” And so I poured all my tea into his pot and it spilled over the floor and into the other room.

        Soon enough the house was flooding and the pot was spilling into the streets and the cracks in the roads. This place with its roads and backyards and woods was being filled with bad tea.

        This place where one could walk and walk and find themselves under the sign of an old bus stop that we’re not really sure serves anymore. And there, it would be raining and it would be late at night and the LED lights on the bus stop would be flickering low and relaxed. And you’d see my roommate with stubble lining his solemn face.

        For everything he has been through, through his journeys meeting princesses in castles and men with pumpkins for heads, for these, he knew where he was, because he had learned, but not because he was told. He just thought I would be there with him, but I am not, and there isn’t any bus coming, and he isn’t my roommate, and soon enough he will be drowning in fairies’ tears.
