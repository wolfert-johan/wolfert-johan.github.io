porcelain steel, or something other, and the embroidering was that which even a Ming Emperor would extol. In my mind's eye I, what was I but eyeing my lies? A skelter brought upon me by dazzling display of homemade beauty intoxicated a pompous poet.

I was lost in blue and white labyrinths, and gliding on the side of China when shot in my breast by a golden sacrament. Cast my running mind and body into rings of green like granny smith. Oh, if only then hindsight had been forthwith! In nuance, I lost recognition of novelty.

And then travesty: my sophistry compelled me ever think a China plate could be recovered or left in a cupboard as such delusion convinces me now that I shall find it again.

How I wished this to be done: like lessons learned in smashing China plates and finding reckless recreation, or rather like the old before reanimate renovation. But now all in thought is reinvitation. Oh, wishful thoughts are knots untied, so fraught are nots when dropped, the time.

Who was I? I left my prayers and went upstairs, ever trapped in whoever’s hairs. Forgetting old things like god. How odd, for did he strike me down? Act me a clown? And if he did, when just a kid, then so was I. But than put old books away, rather would I like to stay, sip some tea with this China plate, know this one, which is so great.
