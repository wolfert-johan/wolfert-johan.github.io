Rose

        Let my blueness be a consequence of my garden, and the garden a consequence of the elevation of my mind. Let that my mind be above the Earth. My blueness is bludgeoning and voluminous, round and draping, consuming and devouring, for my world is Blue.

        Rose, how you stand in my garden, hot as the sun, my crown and centerpiece. Let you be my rose. Your stem and core is green like my victories, my triumphs and pleasures. It is that of you which could not be cut. It is that which I say is you and always will be.

        Rose, your candied fruits be your delusion. Your sweet-looking and supple treats are not your prize, rather they pose, in standoff, retorting disbelief. This yellowness is your cuteness and smallness, your humility and generosity, but these yellow you bare deny your greenness.

Woe, Rose! I tell you not to heed these fruit; how I love to look upon them and deny myself to them. That I laugh at this candy is my gold, and how you love gold, how you hoard it. But Rose, who told you these things? Who would have you craft such wicked and bitter fruit? I hope these shrivel and fall off of you and be replaced by green apples.

Rose, do not have me bite into your bitterness, for what is such yellowness in a blue world but grayness? Grayness is disappointment and pity.

Let me be a lone brain in my garden, so raised above the Earth: a place where ants hustle aimlessly. No ant knows what he does or where he goes, but my garden, a place elevated from these ant brains, has direction. And to be here one must look up! Up! Ants know not of this other dimension, for they only turn back or forward, right or left! Here in my garden I am above the white sun and clouds made for ants, but I may only use the light of these blue skies.

Rose, have you no brain? Why must you overripen? You paint your fruits and petals red with your doings. Red like the color of war and shouting and blood! Woe to you! Oh, but you do and do without a brain. It is a mindless doing! You beg and beg me to be red and enraged like you. Woe to you! Oh, but you do not know how to ask or evoke! Your thorns poke at me and draw my blood. Woe to you! Oh, but a plant grows thorns without a thought.

Do you know of my blueness? Blue is a hammer on my bones and body. Blue is a bruising, until it draws my blood. Blue is a beating on my flesh while I am a mix of arms and legs and ears that it has torn from me. Blue is a devouring of my naiveties and my dreams and my superego. Blue is a denial of my friends and my narratives and my caring. Blue is a wave of apathy and loneliness which swallows me in this garden.

Blue held my hand and befriended me when I was little and ascended to this garden with me, but it grew faster and stronger into a beast and now it has destroyed all of me except my eye. Now I am just an eye, and still, Blue squeezes out of me tears and sorrow that wash away the redness in the blood it drew from me in my youth.

Rose, my world is Blue. How could I see your redness? You will not evoke a redness in me, my blueness routed it a long time ago. I see your redness and it is grayness.

As a sheep by wolves, a track by trains, a rain by storms, a friend by friends, a worker by a communist, a theory by scientists: so am I by blueness. Could I do anything but love my blueness?

Dearest Rose! So your redness and yellowness is grayness in my garden, but what is man without woman? I need help tending to my crops. Besides, is this grayness I see which is drawn from the gold lining my iris, or am I just a Wilson’s patient?

Dearest Rose! May that I only see blueness, then would you let me touch you? Apologies for my grabbing and groping, but so it was that Darwin commanded this, and our bodies listened: I yearn for the serotonergic bliss and variety of touch. Velvet and silk, doughy and coarse, skin and bone, you know it all very well, but do you know what worship a man submerged in blueness can cradle for such newness? How could a monochrome vision outweigh ecstasy?

Dearest Rose! Am I mad to think that in my touching and pushing and pulling that I could dig you out?

Rose! I promise that I will pull you by the stem into this garden!

Rose! I promise that I will hold and study and love you!

Rose! I promise that I will use elevation to know your brain as no other could!
