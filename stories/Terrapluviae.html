Terra pluviae

The rain would fall,

and it would keep falling and it wouldn’t stop.

Like a tired worker striking at nails would the rain fall until,

one day,

there was no one left, knew a world without falling rain.

        In his mother’s arms, there was a boy, a baby. They called him Lilith. Sleeping, his skin was pale, perhaps he was sickly, and being rocked gently.

        Softly, “Lilith, my lovely lilac, my greatest peece, my golden sun, you see me like a reflection now, Your mirror image, but in you is your father, like your stem and heart. Lilith, have the mind of your mother and stand proudly among seas of friends in this world. Lilith, it may be dark and grey, for it forever rains on us, but it is only because we stole the sun from the sky and placed it in you, my dear.”

        A kiss on the forehead.

        Mother was always a strong voice. She boomed nights and days through the small and flat house that was wicked. The thick air stuck to one’s throat like rusty chainmail or wax. Clutter, certainly dragged through centuries and generations of time, collected, made the small rooms feel only even smaller. The intrusivity of the house was not only like a smothering and cuffing of the body, but the oldness of everything found within was a timely arrest which restrained one in the past. Being there felt like waiting for a tired story to repeat itself onto the residents. Noise would rasp at the essence of these whinging halls so that everything seemed to be yelling at whatever or whoever it could get hold of in a high-pitched, nasally tone. It often felt like anything could be heard at any part of the house from any part of the house, though, truly, this was not the case. When mother spoke, though, it was not so nasally and would not ring in your ear. She was not soft, but, indeed she was warm. It made Lilith feel that the hand of the house was one that cupped and did not grope. She made you feel that that repetition of the past which you could not avoid was, at least, leading to a good story, like a party with every dead grandpa and grandma who once lived there.

        Father was a calm voice. On particularly cloudy and foggy days he would walk with Lilith, and his dull eyes wouldn’t help anyone find the way, but when he and he would find themselves lost, it just wasn’t all the bother.

        Looking into Lilith, “So we are lost, boy, and I have lost us, but do you blame me”
        “Yes” with a smile. And Father saw into him and so he was calm, and so then was Lilith.

        Father would then seem a shy soul, and he was, too, for when guests came, he would spend the time where his mouth was open placing food in it, and the time where his mouth was closed chewing it. So a group never knew him, not like a group would know mother. No, and he wasn’t always wise and wasn’t even small, but to a group.

        His past was small, too, for it was hard to imagine Father without Mother, like it is to imagine the purpose of a sink without running water. He didn’t speak of his youth, and God knows if he did exist before entering this house.
